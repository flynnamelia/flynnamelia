Painting

Heritage, 2020.

Acrylic and pen on canvas, 24"x 18"

In this project, I focused on my maternal grandfather's family between 1940 - 1941. When my great-grandfather joined the Royal Canadian Engineers and went overseas during World War II, my great-grandmother purchased an R.C.A, Victor radio. My grandfather has memories of his whole family sitting around the radio listening anxiously to news about the war, music, and radio shows. When my great-grandparents no longer wanted the radio, my grandparents kept it in their house. Eventually when my grandparents no longer wanted it, my own parents took it.
To represent this story, I used the radio as the focal point and included a family photograph taken just before my great-grandfather went overseas. Included as well is a vintage bone china flower bouquet given to my mother as a remembrance of my great-grandfather the year he passed away. I used primarily browns, yellows and golds to capture the warm undertones of the wood and to portray the warmth, depth and richness of my family heritage.

Path at Butchart Gardens, 2020

Acrylic on canvas, 24"x 18"

This work was shown in the KWAG Expressions 45 Digital Exhibit, May 7 - June 30, 2020.
While I was studying the Impressionist painter, Claude Monet, I came across several paintings entitled Garden Path at Giverny which immediately reminded me of a picture I had taken while visiting Butchart Gardens on Vancouver Island on July 15, 2018. I very much wanted to try to capture the colour, light and movement in my photograph the way Monet had done in his garden paintings.

Judy, 2019

Acrylic on poster board.

​I was tasked with recreating a celebrity image in black and white to demonstrate the use of high contrast. As soon as I saw this headshot of Judy Garland, I was flooded with childhood memories of watching the iconic 1939 movie as well as seeing a live theatre production of The Wizard of Oz in Toronto, I just knew I had to paint her. I had originally planned to approach this through the lens of pop art, but I couldn’t resist gravitating towards realism because of how well it portrayed the glowing icon I idolized in childhood.

October, 2019.

Acrylic and watercolour on illustration board, 15"x 20"

In this time capsule painting, all the objects have personal meaning for me and represent the month of October. October was always a fun and exciting time of year when I was growing up. The scarecrow is one of a number of scarecrows that have decorated my house every fall as far back as I can remember along with orangey-yellow sunflowers. The gourds set into a basket represent Thanksgiving and family get-togethers. The pumpkin represents Halloween and all the fun I had dressing up, trick-or-treating and celebrating my brother's and sister’s birthday. My time capsule is now sealed with the orange and purple dried flowers which are recent items to the month of October and come from the floral arrangements present at my father’s funeral.
