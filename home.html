Welcome to my portfolio!

This website is a collection of my artwork from my last two years of high school and my frst two years of University that I created as my final project for my Intro to Web Design and Development course. I've been meaning to update my old art portfolio website that I created back in 2021 to use for university applications for a while now, so when we were asked to create a website that tells the reader about a topic of our choice, such as a hobby or a portfolio, I was excited for an opportunity to check one thing off my to-do list and also apply the skills and knowledge about web design that I've gained over the last twelve weeks. Now that I'm halfway through my undergraduate degree, I hope that this website acts as a reflection of how much both my understanding of art and my artistic skillset has evolved since my final years of high school.

In building this portfolio, it became clear to me that the website I use to display my artwork online is more than just pre-made infrastructure used to house images of my work, like a physical portfolio, a gallery wall or a Wix template. The process of learning how to put together a visual display from textual characters has taught me that the website used to display my artwork can be a work of art in itself. As I wrote lines of HTML to showcase my artwork, I started to question why I had previously understood visual art and computer science as binary opposites. Coding a basic website with HTML and CSS is not entirely unlike painting a picture: basic instruments (like colours of paint or machine-readable elements), when combined with human creativity, produce accessible designs that humans can interpret and appreciate. For me, HTML and CSS aren't only the building blocks of my introduction to web design; they are a gateway into a new medium.

About me:

My name is Amelia and I am a second year student at the University of Guelph majoring in English with minors in Culture and Technology Studies and Studio Art.
Of my many interests, most of my research has focused on digital humanities, women's writing, print history in art and literature, critical theory, comparative literature, and contemporary poetry.
